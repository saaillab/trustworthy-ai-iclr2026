Unifying Perspectives on Learning Biases: A Data-Centric Intervention for Holistic Fairness, Robustness, and Generalization

Patrick Vincent Ndowo 1 , Innocent Nyalala 1,2
1School of Engineering and Science, Indian Institute of Technology Madras Zanzibar, Tanzania
2Wadhwani School of Data Science and AI (WSDSAI), Indian Institute of Technology Madras, Chennai, India
ICLR 2026 Workshop on Principled Design for Trustworthy AI: Interpretability, Robustness, and Safety across Modalities, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil (Apr 2026)
Paper Code BibTeX

Summary: Accepted paper at the ICLR 2026 Trustworthy AI Workshop unifying fairness, robustness, and out-of-distribution generalization via data-centric bias intervention.

Abstract

Fairness, robustness, and out-of-distribution generalization are typically studied in isolation despite sharing common roots in dataset acquisition bias, spurious shortcuts, and representational imbalance. We propose a unified data-centric framework that diagnoses and intervenes on learning biases prior to model fitting, demonstrating simultaneous gains across fairness disparities, environmental perturbations, and cross-domain transfer in resource-constrained deployments.

  1. Formalizes procedural alignment, structuring deep learning pipelines to mirror explicit human segmentation-and-attribute grading stages.
  2. Evaluates 22 architectures across five model families, demonstrating that high clean-set accuracy (99.71%) coincides with negligible explanation faithfulness (2.5-5.8% EER).
  3. Exposes severe domain-shift vulnerability in monolithic black box classifiers (F1 dropping to 31.2-45.8%) compared with 97.2% retained F1 in decomposed architectures.
  4. Unifies fairness, out-of-distribution robustness, and explainability as linked consequences of background shortcut bias.
